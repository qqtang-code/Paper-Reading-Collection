01 · Overview

Overview: what grows does not touch the KV

This paper proposes KITE (KV-Invariant Transformer Expansion), a model-scaling paradigm: when a model is grown from a smaller size, all new capacity is placed in the region that does not determine the attention K/V. As a result, building the KV cache at inference (bulk prefill) only ever runs the original, smaller part of the model; the added "KV-reading" capacity shows up only in decode and in predicting the first output token at the prompt boundary. The concrete instantiation is SST (Step Scale Transformer): two same-depth Transformer towers executed sequentially — a Prefiller that produces layer-wise KV, and a Decoder that reads that KV to predict the next token.

The problem it attacks is a joint one across three cost axes: training compute, prefill compute, and decode compute. Existing routes each carry a debt — progressive upcycling saves training but you then serve the full-size model for less-than-full-size quality; YOCO-style KV reuse saves prefill but can cost quality, and recovering that quality amounts to a training-cost loss. KITE pays all three at once: training is saved (new capacity appears partway through), prefill is saved (the added part does not produce KV), and quality is not conceded (lower training loss at the same compute).

In one line: KITE is not another attention trick — it is an architecture-level answer to "where to grow": no capacity is added to any path that must produce future KV. SST is the plainest possible implementation of that rule (two towers in sequence, layer-aligned KV reuse), and the paper says outright it is probably not the best design — only enough to demonstrate the paradigm. The boundary condition worth memorizing: future KV production must not depend on historical Decoder states; what that buys is prefill that is always priced at the source model's size.
02 · Background & Motivation

Background: saving on one axis is not saving

LLMs exhibit strong scaling behaviour — scale buys capability (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) — and the bill for that scale arrives on both the training and the inference side. Making models larger at lower compute cost is therefore one of the central problems, and the paper stresses that the goal is not merely to spend less: it is to train or serve a stronger model under the same budget.

MoE is the success story here (Switch Transformers, Fedus et al., 2022; Shazeer et al., 2017): at a fixed parameter count, sparse expert routing reduces training and inference computation at once. The paper's point is what that success hides — a saving on only one side may not improve the overall trade-off. Two existing one-sided routes make the contrast concrete:

  • Progressive upcycling (Net2Net: Chen et al., 2016; SPARKLING: Yu et al., 2026): train a small model, grow it into the target partway through, so most tokens run on a cheaper graph. What is saved is training — but upcycled models are generally perceived to underperform same-size models trained from scratch, so the saving is paid back at inference: you serve the full target model and still get less than full target quality.
  • KV reuse (the YOCO family: Sun et al., 2024; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, 2026; KV-Mirror, WeChat AI, 2026): deeper layers reuse keys and values computed by shallower ones, keeping prefill affordable as the model grows. What is saved is inference prefill — but removing KV-producing layers can sacrifice quality, and recovering it typically needs a larger model or longer training, which is effectively a training-cost loss.

KITE's move is to put both accounts on one table: scale the model by expanding only the region that does not affect KV. The added capacity appears partway through training (cheap training), takes no part in KV computation (cheap prefill), and joint training of the two towers absorbs the quality debt. The paper also notes this route is orthogonal to earlier designs such as MoE and hybrid attention — the MoE layers inside SST are the proof by construction.

03 · Observation

Observation: agentic traffic is input-dominated, so prefill is where to save

Why target prefill rather than decode? The argument comes from the shape of real agentic workloads: repeated model calls process accumulated context plus new tool outputs (SWE-agent: Yang et al., 2024; ReAct: Yao et al., 2023), and even with prompt caching a substantial amount of uncached input remains. To quantify this, the paper pulls public OpenRouter model activity (August 22 – September 20, 2026 UTC) for six models:

The accounting is given in Appendix A: with $P$ total input tokens, $C$ cache-read tokens, $U = P - C$ uncached input, and $D$ output tokens (including reasoning), the estimated uncached-input charge $A$ and output charge $O$ combine into a bill share:

$$A = 10^{-6}\sum_v U_v a_v,\qquad O = 10^{-6}\sum_v D_v o_v,\qquad s_{\text{uncached}} = \frac{A}{A+O}. \tag{6}$$

With that weight in hand the paper defines an analytical inference-cost proxy: $\text{proxy} = w\,p + (1-w)\,d$, where $p$ and $d$ are bulk-prefill and decode active body counts normalized to 47B classic, and $w = 0.75$ means a 75:25 P:D mix. Figure 1 uses it to place 0.933 (SST) against 1.365 (63B); §6's Figure 5 sweeps the same weight from 0:100 to 100:0 to see who is cheaper where.

Basis warning [organized]: the paper repeats that these are analytical cost estimates, not measured serving speedups — the proxy only compares how many parameters each token engages, leaving out kernel efficiency, communication, KV memory bandwidth, batching and scheduling. Every "x% lower" on this page inherits that basis.
04 · Method

Method: KITE's principle and the two-tower SST

4.1 The design principle: a structural invariant, not frozen weights

The paper splits a causal model into a Prefiller $P$ that produces reusable attention memory and a Decoder $D$ that predicts:

$$(M_t,\, z_t) = P(x_{1:t};\,\theta_P),\qquad \ell_t = D(x_t,\, z_t,\, M_t;\,\theta_D), \tag{1}$$

Here $M_t$ is the reusable attention memory (the layer-wise K/V), $z_t$ optional Prefiller features at position $t$, and $\ell_t$ the next-token logits. The procedure: finish the small-model (source) stage, then expand $D$ from the intermediate checkpoint while retaining the Prefiller graph and the memory interface; both parts then continue training jointly.

The paper is explicit that the invariant is structural — neither weights nor KV values are frozen. What is constrained are three things:

  • the Decoder must not produce memory that future positions need (K/V comes only from the Prefiller);
  • to skip Decoder history during generation, a retained Decoder position's representation must be independent of the omitted Decoder positions given the Prefiller outputs;
  • bulk prefill — the prompt-wide KV construction performed by the Prefiller — must not grow with the expansion.

Under those restrictions prediction capacity can grow freely while the thing prefill is priced on (tokens × source-tower size) stays fixed. Only two "corners" remain necessary: the readout at the final prompt position for the first output token, and the Decoder's work during decode.

4.2 SST: two same-depth towers, layer-aligned KV reuse

SST instantiates the principle with two Transformer towers (Prefiller and Decoder, 18 layers each in the experiments). The Prefiller runs first and produces layer-wise KV; each Decoder block computes its own query and reads the corresponding Prefiller layer's KV:

$$q^{D}_{l,t} = Q^{D}_{l}\!\left(h^{D}_{l-1,t}\right),\qquad a^{D}_{l,t} = \mathrm{Attn}\!\left(q^{D}_{l,t},\; K^{P}_{l}\!\left[I_l(t)\right],\; V^{P}_{l}\!\left[I_l(t)\right]\right). \tag{2}$$

where $Q^D_l$ includes query projection and normalization and $I_l(t)$ is the set of positions allowed by the original causal full/sliding mask. Both towers keep the source model's position ids. The Decoder has independent block parameters, and its residual and FFN/MoE operations are token-local — that is the root reason its historical positions can be omitted.

The entry bridge and the readout are deliberately lightweight. For the shared embedding/stem $e_t$ and the final Prefiller state $h^{P}_{L,t}$, the entry bridge is a sum of parameter-free RMS operations:

$$h^{D}_{0,t} = \mathrm{RMS}_\epsilon(e_t) + \mathrm{RMS}_\epsilon\!\left(h^{P}_{L,t}\right),\qquad \mathrm{RMS}_\epsilon(v) = \frac{v}{\sqrt{\mathrm{mean}(v^2)+\epsilon}}, \tag{3}$$

with $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$. The Decoder's final state produces the logits through the shared output normalization and head:

$$\ell_t = W_{\text{out}}\,\mathrm{Norm}_{\text{out}}\!\left(h^{D}_{L,t}\right). \tag{4}$$

The two towers share one copy of the embedding, output normalization and output head (embedding and head weights are untied). Gradients reach the Prefiller along two paths — the entry bridge and the reused KV. Given the Prefiller outputs, each Decoder position is independent of earlier Decoder states, which is what makes "prefill computes only the final position" a mathematical property rather than an engineering approximation.

Walkthrough [estimated]: where 66.959 and 2.155 come from. Treat SST as "the source tower twice, minus the shared pieces kept once, minus what the Decoder does not need": $2 \times 33.819 = 67.638$; subtract the duplicated embedding/head ($0.594$, kept once) to get $67.044$; then subtract the K/V projections and K normalization the Decoder lacks — roughly $2 \times 2304 \times (8 \times 128) \approx 4.72$M parameters per layer, $\times 18$ layers $\approx 85$M — landing on $66.959$, matching Table 6. The same 85M explains the active counts: SST decode active $2.155 = 1.120$ (Prefiller) $+ 1.035$ (Decoder = the source's 1.120 minus the 0.085 it does not carry), while prefill active is simply $1.120$. The paper only publishes the end numbers (Tables 2/5/6 plus Appendix B's conventions); this decomposition is this page's reading of that basis, offered for cross-checking.

Finally, the execution in three stages (§2.3 — the paragraph most worth memorizing):

  • Training. After expansion both towers train jointly: the Prefiller processes the full sequence, the Decoder computes every supervised position, and gradients flow into the Prefiller through the reused KV as well as through the two entry paths ($e$ and $h^P_L$).
  • Prefill. The Prefiller processes the full prompt $x_{1:n}$ and produces the layer-wise KV cache; the Decoder needs to compute only the final position $n$ (attending to Prefiller KV) to predict the first output token — earlier Decoder positions are omitted entirely because its operations are token-local.
  • Decode. Each new token passes through both towers: the Prefiller extends the KV cache and the Decoder reads it to form the next-token prediction.
In one line: the design decouples expansion from KV construction — new capacity exists as a Decoder tower that only adds decode and prompt-boundary work, while the Prefiller stays bit-for-bit aligned with the source so the KV cache is structurally identical. The price is stated plainly: decode gets heavier (1.12B → 2.155B), which is why the bet is on prefill-dominated workloads (§3 and Figure 5).
05 · Training & Systems

Training and systems: how the expansion is wired, and how the budget is counted

5.1 Expansion and continued training

The expansion itself is plain: each source layer is copied into the corresponding layers of the Prefiller and the Decoder, and the two towers share a single copy of the embedding, output normalization and output head. Both towers then train jointly in BF16, reusing the source model's Muon (Liu et al., 2025) and Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2014) parameter groups: optimizer states are retained for the Prefiller and the shared parameters, and initialized fresh for the Decoder; all groups continue on the source's cosine learning-rate schedule, with the remaining decay extended over the continuation stage and no separate warmup for the Decoder (details in Appendix D).

5.2 Cumulative training compute: how three models end up "comparable"

The paper aligns budgets in theoretical FLOPs. For source and continuation token counts $D_s$, $D_c$ and forward FLOPs/token $f_s$, $f_c$ (all under the effective architecture):

$$C_{\text{train}} = 3\,(D_s f_s + D_c f_c), \tag{5}$$

where the factor of three approximates forward-plus-backward. The evaluated 47B classic checkpoint defines the reference $B_{\text{ref}} \approx 5.16836\times 10^{21}$ FLOPs, with forward coefficients of 3.887488B (47B), 5.148513B (63B), 3.087252B (source) and 5.410678B (SST) FLOPs/token — SST keeps both towers' attention and FFN/MoE but only one embedding, output head and set of KV projections.

Cross-check [estimated]: recompute with Equation (5) and the paper's coefficients — SST: $3\times(167.98\text{B}\times 3.087252 + 222.55\text{B}\times 5.410678) \approx 3\times(5.186\times10^{20} + 1.2042\times10^{21}) \approx 5.168\times10^{21}$; 47B: $3\times 443.16\text{B}\times 3.887488 \approx 5.168\times10^{21}$; 63B: $3\times 334.62\text{B}\times 5.148513 \approx 5.168\times10^{21}$. All three land within a tenth of a percent of $B_{\text{ref}}$ — "comparable cumulative training compute" is a computed match, not a loose claim.

5.3 Recipe and the continuation scheduler

Appendix D supplies the reproducibility-relevant settings: continuation runs on 128 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, expert parallelism 8, sequence length 4,096, global batch 3,072 sequences; the source peak base learning rate is about $1.03302\times10^{-3}$; the continuation base weight decay is 0.15 with inherited parameter-group multipliers; the source/SST/63B token batch is 12,582,912 (14,680,064 for the 47B). Muon uses six Newton–Schulz steps with packed attention split by head and GLU split by projection type; Adam uses $\epsilon = 10^{-15}$. The entry and final-state RMS operations accumulate in FP32 and return the input dtype.

The continuation learning rate does not re-warm-up; it resumes the source cosine phase. With $u$ the continuation tokens consumed, $U = 222{,}553{,}964{,}544$ the configured continuation horizon, and $q_0$ the source's normalized cosine phase at conversion:

$$q(u) = q_0 + (1-q_0)\min(u/U,\,1), \tag{7}$$
$$\eta(u) = \eta_{\min} + \left(\eta_{\text{anchor}} - \eta_{\min}\right)\frac{1+\cos\!\left(\pi q(u)\right)}{1+\cos(\pi q_0)}, \tag{8}$$

with $\eta_{\text{anchor}} \approx 5.96548\times10^{-4}$ and $\eta_{\min} \approx 1.03302\times10^{-4}$. The source schedule warms up over 12.582912B tokens and decays until 335.9605248B; conversion occurs at 167.9818752B tokens — and the paper notes explicitly that $q_0$ is measured within the post-warmup decay interval, not by dividing the conversion position by the whole horizon.

Reproducibility view [organized]: the paper discloses kit-level settings (GPU count, parallelism, batch, optimizers, LR schedule, token counts) but says only that the data recipe is "the same" as the baselines, without a mix; and a 128×H800 run is not something a third party reproduces cheaply. The evidence is therefore a controlled comparison inside the authors' own ladder, not an independently reproducible experiment — picked up again in §8.

07 · Design Space & Related Work

Design space: SST is one point in it

The discussion chapter lays out both the boundary and the degrees of freedom: SST is one realization of KITE, not a unique prescription. Its aligned layer-wise KV reuse, entry bridge and Decoder-only readout are specific choices; the central requirement is only that the added capacity must not extend the KV-producing computation or introduce dependencies on historical Decoder states.

Placing SST on the map of prior work (§6, citations in the paper's own terms):

  • Model growth. Net2Net (Chen et al., 2016) studies function-preserving growth; SPARKLING (Yu et al., 2026) examines signal preservation and symmetry breaking in width-progressive learning. SST differs by not being function-preserving: aligned initialization changes the prediction graph, and joint adaptation afterwards pays for it. Dense-to-MoE upcycling (Sparse Upcycling: Komatsuzaki et al., 2022; He et al., 2024) turns a dense checkpoint into sparse experts, whereas SST adds a Decoder tower to a source that is already a sparse MoE.
  • The KV-shrinking family. MQA (Shazeer, 2019) and GQA (Ainslie et al., 2023) reduce stored attention state by sharing KV heads; Cross-Layer Attention (Brandon et al., 2024) extends sharing across adjacent layers. The paper's positioning: SST does not shrink KV — it expands capacity without extending the KV-producing path.
  • The YOCO family. YOCO (Sun et al., 2024) separates cache-producing from cache-consuming computation and exploits it for prefill early exit; SST applies that execution pattern to staged model growth — YOCO's cross-decoder shares one global KV representation, while SST reads aligned layer-wise KV and is constructed by source-to-target expansion. Also in this family: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (2026, exhibit ⑥ in this collection) and KV-Mirror (WeChat AI, 2026).
  • Recursion / weight tying. Mixture-of-Recursions (Bae et al., 2025) explores recursive KV sharing; from the architectural view, fixed-KV looping can be read as a parameter-tied special case of the family KITE belongs to. SST instead uses independent Prefiller and Decoder parameters, expanding capacity without extending the KV-producing path.
In one line: this chapter upgrades "a model" into "a searchable design space" — KV assignment, hidden-state connections, bridge operators and the capacity split between towers are all open dimensions. The practical takeaway: if you work on YOCO-style or CLA-style architectures, the constraint "expand only on the consuming side" transfers directly.

8.2 Three things this paper does well

  • It makes "where to grow" a first-class question. Most expansion/upcycling work asks how much; this one asks which region can grow without breaking what. The KV invariant is a reusable architectural constraint, not just one diagram.
  • It reports all three cost axes, transparently. The training budget includes the source stage (390.54B tokens), $B_{\text{ref}}$ and all four forward coefficients are published, and the recomputation checks out (§5.2). On the inference side it uses an analytical proxy and says so repeatedly — considerably more honest than a vague "faster".
  • The ladder is cleanly controlled. Same tokenizer, data recipe, protocol and evaluation sets; the 47B/63B baselines come from the same family by depth/width and SST sits on top of it — separating the scaling paradigm from the model-family variable. Seven downstream tasks plus held-out NLL all point the same way.

8.3 Not disclosed, worth testing next

  • No measured serving speedup. The whole inference case is the 0.75p+0.25d proxy; there is no end-to-end throughput, TTFT/TPOT or memory measurement. SST executes asymmetrically (prefill on the Prefiller, decode on both towers), so cache layout, scheduling granularity and kernel shapes all change in a real engine — worth measuring how much of the "parameters saved at prefill" actually converts into wall-clock on vLLM or SGLang. The paper's own conclusion stops at "without establishing ... measured serving speedup".
  • No sensitivity scan over the expansion point. The 167.98B / 222.55B split (43:57) is given; there is no sweep of "when to expand" or "how much to expand", and no side-by-side of SST-style loss-compute curves for different conversion points — arguably the paradigm's most important hyperparameter.
  • Capacity allocation unexplored. Equal depth and width for both towers (18 layers / 2304 each) is a design choice, and the paper says outright that optimal connectivity and capacity allocation remain open. A shallower or narrower Decoder, or the Reversed / Shared source assignments of §5, are the obvious next questions.
  • Single runs and statistical basis. All numbers come from one training run: the loss margin over 63B (0.0021) and small downstream gaps (e.g. BBH +0.18) have no seeds or repeats behind them, and evaluation sampling details beyond shot counts are not disclosed.
  • The two-tower KV/memory account is not laid out. By construction the Decoder produces no KV, so the cache should be source-sized — but the paper gives no actual KV-cache size, activation memory or prefill batching efficiency. For practitioners, those decide whether an analytical saving becomes an engineering one. Also: the data recipe is only "the same" as the baselines, without a mixture.

8.4 One-sentence verdict

This is a paradigm paper: its real contribution is the constraint ("expansion must not touch KV") plus one simple, transparently accounted instantiation (SST) that shows all three axes moving the same way at one compute point. Its weaknesses are equally clear — every "cheaper" is analytical, and every "better" comes from a single run on one ladder. Read it as method: whenever part of a model grows, ask first whether it sits on the path that must produce future state.
